Copy framework — the ABOUT template

Decision needed from Dylan · reverse-engineered from 8 real-site teardowns · becomes the skeleton for the ABOUT, then the whole site · full working doc: docs/copy/ABOUT-TEMPLATE.md
The question you asked, answered honestly: a full first-person founder memoir on a company's own About page is not a classic-SaaS thing — it's a bootstrapper/indie move. The one variable that predicts who shows the founder: whether the company already has external proof (logos, big numbers, press) to trust instead. Tideover has none of that yet — which is exactly why some founder presence is necessary here.

Read the pattern: only Basecamp (a bootstrapper) runs a big founder story on its own About. The creator brands that have one (Kit, Gumroad) hide it on a personal domain. Classic B2B/fintech (Linear, Stripe, Mercury) and even DTC Oatly keep the founder minimal-to-absent — because they own proof Tideover doesn't. Honest correction: Oatly, the DTC example, is not a founder-story brand at all — so "big DTC founder story" is a weaker convention than it feels.
Verdict for Tideover: founder-led sale, no case studies → the founder's lived experience is the only credibility asset on hand, so some founder presence is necessary and earns trust. But it need not be a memoir — a compact first-person letter (~450–650 words), not Gumroad's 7,400. Put the named founder + the lived wait up front; prove trust with admission + structural fact + exact self-owned numbers; then stop.

Recommended template site

Basecamp / 37signals — "Where we came from"

  • It's the only model that manufactures trust with zero case studies — the About is a first-person admission ("clients noticed our failure first"), not a results wall. That is Tideover's exact problem, already solved.
  • Its three proof mechanisms are all things Tideover already owns: narrative admission, structural fact (how you operate, not how big you are), and opinion-as-hype-substitute — none need scale, logos, or numbers Tideover lacks. The founder-minimal sites prove trust with exactly the assets Tideover doesn't have.
  • The register is the target voice: plain-spoken, problem-first, low-hype, conviction-over-superlatives — a bootstrapper talking, not a VC brand performing. Maps 1:1 onto "calm, trust-first."
Runner-up: PostHog — same named-and-photographed founder-as-stamp instinct plus confess-then-convert and transparency-as-proof, but with the argument kept institutional (better for the home / how-it-works pages where a full letter is too much). Adopt its structure; strip its cocky tone. Two grafts onto the Basecamp spine: Stripe's honest-limits block replaces Basecamp's revenue-proof section (Tideover has no revenue fact yet), and Gumroad's confession-first open names the gap before any claim.

The ABOUT template — section-by-section skeleton

Basecamp's 8-part arc, adapted to proof-only. Copy stays bracketed until the framework is locked. Total target ~450–650 words. Full "Says" + structural moves in docs/copy/ABOUT-TEMPLATE.md.

SectionWordsFunctionSays (describing words — not the copy)
1 · Thesis line~10–15Frame the story as necessity, not geniusOne flat fragment naming the wait as the reason the company exists. No product, no benefit. Pattern: "Born out of desperate necessity."
2 · Founder intro~35–60Put a named, real human on the page at onceWarm "I'm Dylan" + the one load-bearing credential — he lived the exact wait he now sells relief from. Photo here. No résumé.
3 · The lived problem~140–200Earn trust by admitting the pain — replaces case studiesThe surge that looked like a win, the 60+ day wait he couldn't move, the WISMO inbox, the toll. Dated, concrete, exact self-owned numbers. Candid, low-drama.
4 · The turning point~70–110Turn the pain into the idea worth solvingThe "magic wand" moment in his words → where Tideover (the literal name) was born. Reflective, quiet — the hinge of the letter.
5 · What got built~110–170Show the product emerging from the scar; judgment turned into softwareInstinct fails at the 300th 11pm email → so it's in code: replies audited, hard dates blocked, results vs your own baseline. Pragmatic, concrete, no hype.
6 · Honest limits + proof-only pledge~80–120Convert the absence of proof into the reason to trustProof only: no fake metrics, no logo wall, no line he can't back; his numbers are his own account until real merchant results replace them. Direct, transparent — honesty as a flex. (graft: Stripe)
7 · Who it's for + invitation~70–110Name the exact reader, open a real doorShopify merchants in a 60–120 day wait after a campaign overperformed → a small founding-partner group, direct access, results become proof only with sign-off. Warm, specific, low-pressure.
8 · Signature + human door~15–30Close on a person, not a brandOne line to the merchant mid-onslaught → "— Dylan" + a real reply-to. Quiet, direct, human.

Voice rules (proof-only, distilled from Basecamp)

1 · Write like one email to one person — first-person, contractions, zero corporate mission-speak.   2 · Open on the problem or a plain fact, never the product.   3 · Admission before claim — name the gap first.   4 · Opinion is the hype-substitute — short beliefs, no superlatives.   5 · Proof = narrative + structural fact + exact numbers only — never scale flexes or borrowed logos.   6 · Every number exact and self-owned, flagged as his own account until real results replace it.   7 · Short declaratives; sign with a real name and a real door.

Same 7 voice rules carry over unchanged.
