What changed since v1, and why

Your Scale objection was right, and the research says why it read wrong. Gorgias sells a bigger jump than ours ($360 Pro to $900 Advanced, +150%) and gets away with it because the jump buys a hard published cap increase: 2,000 to 5,000 tickets, printed on the card (gorgias.com/pricing). v1 named the meter but printed no per-tier numbers, so $899 was +$400 for a feature list. The fix is threefold:
  1. Scale drops $899 → $749 and gets loaded with differentiators that are already spec'd (unlimited evidence packs, QA scorecard, assignment routing, priority SLA, API). The gap becomes +$250 (50%), matching the Starter→Growth step.
  2. Order-volume caps now print on every card. You liked metering on total order volume; the caps are what make each rung self-justify. A merchant picks a band from their own order book, so the bill is known in advance and never spikes on a bad week.
  3. Credits got a full evaluation and an honest verdict below: no as the core meter, yes as a dollar wallet for gifts only.
Unchanged: the floor. Founding partners convert at $199–299/mo, "the lowest I'll offer," in writing. No public tier sits below $299. Managed tiers unchanged and approved.

Soft limits, always (the Swan lesson, inverted)

  • Crossing a cap never pauses anything. Swan's automations stop at zero credits (salesforge review); tolerable for outbound, fatal at day 45 of a 90-day presale wait. Tideover degrades never, bills later: we finish your cycle at the current price and have an upgrade conversation.
  • No overage penalty. Swan prices top-ups at 150% of plan rate; Gorgias charges $36–40 per 100 extra tickets. A Kickstarter graduate spikes by design; punishing the spike punishes the moment of highest need.
  • Passive access is free (Swan's one universally good mechanic): unlimited seats, your VA works in the cockpit free, end customers never count toward anything.
  • Pause between campaign cycles. Presale is seasonal; billing respects that. This is our answer to rollover-fairness copy without inventing a currency to roll over.

Should Tideover use credits? Honest verdict: no as the meter, yes as a wallet

The meta is real: 79 of the PricingSaaS 500 now offer a credit model, up from 35 at end of 2024, +126% in a year (Growth Unhinged, 2025 State of SaaS Pricing). But the reason credits swept AI GTM does not apply to us, and the same analyst's 2026 call is that the pendulum swings back to simplicity.
  1. Credits are margin insurance for vendors with per-action COGS. Swan pays for enrichment lookups; Clay pays data providers per lookup (clay.com/pricing). Tideover's marginal cost per order-in-window is cents. Importing a credit meter imports the anxiety without protecting anything.
  2. It torches our wedge. The cold-outreach line is "one flat fee, no per-resolution meter, no setup fee" against Gorgias's double meter (ticket tiers plus $1.00 per AI resolution, gorgias.com/pricing). You cannot send that email and also sell credits.
  3. Value anti-correlation. Any per-action meter bills the merchant most exactly when a delay hits and tickets spike, which is when they are most cash-poor. Intercom's $0.99/resolution works because Fin's resolutions ARE the value (intercom.com/pricing); Tideover's value is the tickets that never arrive, which is unmeterable.
  4. The failure modes are on the record: Clay's unpredictable bills and pay-for-the-attempt charging (eesel.ai teardown), Cursor's opaque usage pool and public CEO apology with refunds (TechCrunch), Apollo's use-it-or-lose-it expiry resentment (eesel.ai).

The one credit-shaped thing that fits: the Goodwill Wallet

Exactly one Tideover action has real per-unit cost: gift sends (physical or monetary goodwill to an angry waiting customer). For that, a prepaid wallet, available at every tier:

  • Denominated in dollars, not an invented currency. Lemlist pins 1 credit = $0.01 for legibility (lemlist.com/pricing); we go one further and just use dollars.
  • Gift cost passes through 1:1 plus a flat handling fee per send ($0 during pilots). Charged only on successful delivery (Lemlist's pay-per-success answer to Clay's pay-for-the-attempt complaint).
  • Never expires while subscribed; refundable on exit. Matches the delete-everything-on-exit doctrine and dodges the Apollo/Cursor rollover resentment on record.
  • Balance renders on the plan-usage dashboard tile already spec'd.
Do NOT meter evidence packs or social alerts. A dispute already costs the merchant ~$15–25 in fees before product loss; charging $5 per pack at their worst moment reads as petty and kills the "we're on your side of the chargeback" story. Pack quantity and alert depth are tier differentiators instead, and that is precisely what manufactures the Scale-tier justification v1 was missing.

The rules the page states out loud (each one a cited wedge)

  • Flat price. No per-ticket meter, no per-AI-resolution fee (Gorgias bills both, on their own pricing page)
  • No setup fee (TalentPop charges ~$2,500)
  • No credits, no overage penalties, nothing ever pauses (Swan: 150% top-up rate, automations stop at zero)
  • Unlimited seats; your VA works in the cockpit free
  • Month-to-month; pause between campaign cycles
  • No performance fees until real case studies exist (proof-only)
