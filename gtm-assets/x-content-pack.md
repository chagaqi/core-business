### Week 1 — name the problem

**D1 · single · [CATEGORY]** (from W1-P1)
> There's a ~$7.5B industry with no name for its most expensive moment.
>
> Kickstarter has funded $7.5B+ across 250k projects. Most of those creators end up on Shopify running preorders.
>
> They all hit the same wall the day the money clears: the 60–120 days between "charged" and "shipped."
>
> Nobody sells anything for that window. Preorder apps quit at the sale. Helpdesks can't tell a preorder from a normal order. So it sits there turning happy buyers into chargebacks.
>
> It's not a logistics problem. It's a comms problem in a logistics costume.

**D2 · single · [WAR STORY]** (from W1-P2)
> I once got a "where's my order, you scammers" email at 11pm. Three weeks into an 8-week lead time.
>
> The product page said the lead time. So did the confirmation email. So did the FAQ.
>
> Didn't matter. People don't read the timeline. They feel the wait.
>
> "You're right on schedule" calms a buyer at day 12 and enrages them at day 60. The reply has to know where the buyer is in the wait. Almost none of them do.

**D3 · THREAD · [METHOD]** (from W1-P3)
> 1/ A presale buyer is 4 different people over one wait. Most brands answer all 4 with the same canned reply. That's the whole problem.
>
> 2/ Day 7–14. Curious. "Did my order go through?" They need a calm confirmation and a clear ship window, so they stop refreshing.
>
> 3/ Day 30–45. Restless. "Is this actually happening?" Silence breeds doubt. They need a real production-stage update, not "thanks for your patience."
>
> 4/ Day 60–75. Frustrated. "I want a refund." The canned line makes it worse. Acknowledge the wait, show movement, offer one concrete next step.
>
> 5/ Day 85–120. Dispute-risk. "Last chance before I charge back." The hardest save. Honesty, real progress, a reason to wait 24 more hours.
>
> 6/ Same customer. Four scripts. One inbox treating them identically. Which stage is your support quietly losing people at?

**D4 · single · [CONTRARIAN]** (from W1-P4)
> "Just put the timeline everywhere on your site" is the most useless preorder advice on the internet.
>
> It's the top reply in every thread. Someone's drowning in where's-my-order tickets and the answer is always "make the lead time more visible."
>
> They already did. Buyers don't email because they missed the date. They email because the wait got uncomfortable and they need a human to make it okay.
>
> You can't fix a feelings problem with a bigger font.

### Week 2 — why the tools fail

**D6 · single · [CONTRARIAN]** (from W2-P1)
> Your $200/mo helpdesk has no idea which of your orders are preorders. That's the whole problem.
>
> Gorgias, Tidio, Intercom are good tools. But they were built for a world where "where's my order" has a tracking-number answer. Preorder breaks that.
>
> Real quote from the Gorgias community: "we're guessing." Real quote from Tidio's: the bot "gives standard shipping answers even though it's a pre-order that hasn't shipped."
>
> The agent guesses. The bot is confidently wrong. Confidently wrong is worse than silent.

**D7 · single · [CATEGORY]** (from W2-P2)
> Crowdfunding graduates have a support problem nobody warned them about: they're running three timelines at once.
>
> KS funds. You open BackerKit late-pledges. Then you move to Shopify and open fresh preorders.
>
> Three customer groups, three "when do I get it" answers, one inbox that can't tell them apart. BackerKit handled the updates for you. The day you leave it, all of that becomes raw email, and your buyers think they're normal orders that are just late.
>
> This is where most crowdfunding brands' support quietly falls apart.

**D8 · THREAD · [METHOD]** (from W2-P3)
> 1/ The best message in a preorder isn't a reply. It's the one you send before they ask.
>
> 2/ Reactive support waits for the where's-my-order email, then answers. Fine. But you've already paid the anxiety tax by the time it lands.
>
> 3/ ~Day 30: "Your batch moved into production. Still on track for your weeks 9–11 window. No action needed, just didn't want you wondering."
>
> 4/ ~Day 60: "Halfway check-in. Real status + a photo of the production run. You're in good shape."
>
> 5/ ~Day 85: "Almost there. Final QC this week, tracking generates next. You'll have it the second it's live."
>
> 6/ Each message kills the reason for ~10 inbound emails. You're not adding work. You're moving it earlier, where it's one message instead of fifty replies.

**D9 · single · [WAR STORY]** (from W2-P4)
> A chargeback on a preorder costs you three times. Most founders count it once.
>
> 1. The dispute fee ($15–25, per card-network ranges).
> 2. The product, which often ships anyway because production already moved.
> 3. Your account health. Enough disputes and your processor starts watching you.
>
> Founders obsess over the fee and miss that #3 is the one that ends brands. The cheapest prevention there is: a calm, timely answer before day 89.

**D10 · single · [BUILD-IN-PUBLIC]** (from W2-P5)
> I'm deliberately not building the obvious version of this.
>
> Obvious move: slick autonomous bot, point it at preorder customers, let it fire replies, charge $499, ship in two weeks.
>
> A bot that invents a ship date to calm someone down just made a written promise you can't keep. That feeds the exact dispute you were trying to prevent, and it's far worse on a preorder than on in-stock support.
>
> So: concierge-first. I draft, the merchant approves, then it sends. Never an invented date. The AI was never the hard part. Knowing what to say at day 60 is.

### Week 3 — the method, deepened

**D11 · THREAD · [METHOD]** (from W3-P1)
> 1/ There are exactly 5 messages that decide whether a preorder ends in a delivery or a dispute.
>
> 2/ The confirmation (day 0–2). Set the confidence-band window, promise proactive updates. Most brands nail this, then go silent.
>
> 3/ The "still alive" update (~day 30). Cheapest trust-builder there is. Most brands skip it.
>
> 4/ The mid-wait proof (~day 60). Show a real production stage. Doubt either dies here or metastasizes.
>
> 5/ The frustration save (~day 60–75). "I want a refund." Acknowledge, show movement, one next step.
>
> 6/ The dispute interception (~day 85–120). "Last chance before I charge back." Honesty, real progress, a reason to wait 24 hours.
>
> 7/ Miss #2 and #3 and you guarantee yourself a flood of #4 and #5. Which of the five does your brand actually send?

**D12 · single · [CATEGORY]** (from W3-P2)
> Let's name the metric every preorder brand bleeds from and can't see: Presale Support Load.
>
> It's the support cost generated after the sale, during the wait, per preorder. Nobody tracks it because it hides inside general support volume. For a long-wait brand it's enormous.
>
> "Pre-orders are good for cash flow but the support cost is insane. We're basically copy-pasting the same response all day." (Shopify Entrepreneurs)
>
> Tag your tickets `preorder-wait` for a week. The number will shock you. That's the first step to reducing it.

**D13 · single · [WAR STORY]** (from W3-P3)
> The line that saved more preorders for me than any discount or apology: "You won't have to ask again."
>
> A 10%-off "sorry for the wait" code reads as guilt and makes people more suspicious something's wrong.
>
> People email twice a day because they've learned that if they don't poke you, they hear nothing. So I promised: "I'll message you the moment it moves to production. You won't have to ask again." Then I actually did. Day 30. Day 60.
>
> The follow-ups dried up. Reassurance is a system of kept promises. Tone is just how you deliver it.

**D14 · single · [CONTRARIAN]** (from W3-P4)
> Preorder refunds aren't a trust problem. They're a clock problem. Big difference.
>
> The standard story: people refund because they don't trust the brand, so pour energy into badges and reviews. It misses the mechanism.
>
> Watch a real cohort and disputes aren't random. They cluster at specific points in the wait, and spike hardest where the brand went quiet. Trust isn't built once at checkout. It decays over the wait unless you top it up.
>
> Good news: clocks are predictable. Put a message at day X-minus-5 and you flatten the spike. You can't out-badge a clock. You out-communicate it.
