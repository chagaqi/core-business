# The Fulfillment Update Template Pack

**Paste-ready messages for the wait between "funded" and "delivered."**

Written by someone who ran fulfillment and the where's-my-order inbox for a physical-goods brand through two years of 60-to-120-day waits. Every template here is one I'd have sent, or did. No hard dates, no invented tracking numbers, nothing you can't stand behind when it slips.

Bracketed fields `[like this]` are the only text you change per backer. Read each one aloud before you send it. If it sounds like a corporate autoresponder, it'll read like one.

---

## The four rules every template below obeys

You can throw out the wording and keep these. They're the whole craft.

1. **Acknowledge the wait before you explain it.** The backer already knows it's late. Leading with the reason reads as a defense. Leading with "you've been waiting, I see it" reads as a person.
2. **Give an ETA as a band, never a hard date.** "The next few weeks" survives a slip. "March 14th" becomes the screenshot in a chargeback. A date you miss costs you more trust than the wait ever did.
3. **Never claim what you can't prove.** No tracking number until one exists. No "on schedule" when you don't control the schedule. No "nothing's slipped" to a person whose order slipped. One invented fact, caught once, and every future update reads as spin.
4. **The day-20 answer is not the day-80 answer.** The wording that calms someone early reads as a brush-off late. Early: reassure. Late: level with them. Most disputes come from sending the early answer to a late backer.

The five templates marked ★ are the core pack. The rest are the situations those five don't cover.

## Timing: the wait, as it lengthens

### ★ 1. Early check-in (roughly day 5–15)

> Hi [firstName] — thanks for backing [product]. Totally fair to want to know where things stand this early.
>
> Right now [what's actually happening, e.g. "components are being sourced"]. The current window for delivery is [ETA band, e.g. "around 10–12 weeks out"], and I'll message you the moment that moves.
>
> Nothing you need to do. I've got it from here.

**Why it's worded this way.** Early, the backer isn't worried yet, they're curious. You're setting the relationship: I'll come to you before you have to come to me. The specific stage ("components are being sourced") is doing the reassurance, not an adjective like "excited." Give them a real thing and a real band.

### 2. The one-month update (roughly day 30)

> Hi [firstName] — a month in, and I want you to have a real update, not a form reply.
>
> Where it actually is: [current stage]. Current window is [ETA band]. If anything changes, you'll hear it from me first, not from a shipping notification that never comes.
>
> Appreciate you sticking with it.

**Why.** Month one is where the first doubt shows up. The move is to prove you're tracking their specific order, not sending a blast. "You'll hear it from me first" is a promise you can actually keep, so make it.

### 3. The two-month check-in (roughly day 60)

> Totally fair to feel the wait at the two-month mark, [firstName]. You've been patient and I don't take it for granted.
>
> Here's where things stand: [current stage]. Window from here is [ETA band]. The moment there's tracking to send, it's yours.
>
> One thing I can do now: flag your order for priority dispatch when the batch lands, so you're in the first wave out. Want me to?

**Why.** By month two, patience is a resource you're spending down. The priority-dispatch offer costs you almost nothing and hands them control back. A backer who's been given a small yes is far less likely to open a dispute.

### ★ 4. The long wait, straight (roughly day 89 and past)

> [firstName] — you've waited longer than anyone should have to for this, and I'm not going to hand you a canned line.
>
> Where it actually stands: [current stage, or "I'm getting the current status from the factory and will have it to you by [day]" if you genuinely don't know]. As straight as I can put the timing: [ETA band].
>
> I'm not going to invent a tracking number I don't have. I'd rather sort this out with you directly than have it go to your card issuer. I'm on it personally, and you can reply straight to me.

**Why.** This is the email that decides whether you get a chargeback. The only thing that works this late is dropping the reassurance voice entirely and being a person who's accountable. Offering the direct line before they think of their bank is the whole play. Note what it never does: promise a date, or claim a status you can't confirm.

## Events: when something changes

### ★ 5. Delay announcement (proactive, when a date is going to slip)

> Hi [firstName] — straight up: [product] is running behind the window I gave you. I'd rather tell you now than let the old date pass in silence.
>
> What happened: [one honest sentence, e.g. "the factory's QC caught a defect in the first run and we're re-doing it rather than ship you a unit that fails"]. What it means for you: the new window is [ETA band].
>
> I know a delay isn't what you wanted to read. If you'd rather not wait, reply and we'll talk through your options. Otherwise I'll have the next update to you by [day, not a delivery date, an update date].

**Why.** Announcing a slip before the date passes is the single highest-trust move in fulfillment, and almost nobody does it. The reason is one honest sentence, and QC-caught-a-defect is a reason that makes you look better, not worse. Always pair the bad news with a next-*update* date you control, so the silence doesn't restart.

### 6. Missed estimated delivery date (the EDD came and went)

> [firstName] — the delivery estimate I gave you was [timeframe], and that's passed. That's on me to address, not on you to chase.
>
> Current status: [stage]. Honest revised window: [ETA band]. I'm not going to give you another date I'm not sure of, so I'll come back to you [day] with the next real update regardless of whether it's moved.

**Why.** When you've already missed once, your credibility on dates is spent. So you stop trading in dates and start trading in update-commitments, which you can keep. "Regardless of whether it's moved" removes their reason to sit and refresh their inbox.

### 7. Proof-of-life update (keep a quiet backer warm)

> Hi [firstName] — no action needed, just proof it's real and moving.
>
> [One concrete thing, e.g. "the first production photos came in this week" or "your batch cleared customs Tuesday"]. Still on track for the [ETA band] window.
>
> More when there's more. Thanks for the patience.

**Why.** The wound in a long wait is silence, not the wait itself. A ten-second update with one concrete detail resets the clock on their anxiety. Send these unprompted. They're the cheapest chargeback insurance you have.

### 8. Gone-quiet re-engagement (a backer who stopped replying, or you went dark)

> [firstName] — it's been a while since I've had something worth sending, and I don't want the quiet to read as gone.
>
> Where [product] actually is: [stage]. Window from here: [ETA band]. Still very much happening, still yours, and I'll be back with the next update by [day].
>
> If anything's changed on your end, just reply.

**Why.** Silence gets filled with the worst assumption, usually "I've been scammed." Breaking it yourself, and naming the silence, defuses that before it hardens into a dispute or a public post.

## Severity: when they're upset

### ★ 9. Refund request

> Hi [firstName] — I hear you, and asking for a refund after this wait is completely reasonable.
>
> [If you offer refunds: "I can process that. Give me [timeframe] and it's back on your original payment method, no hassle."]
>
> [If you don't, and said so upfront: "Here's where I'm at, straight: [product] is [stage] and the window from here is [ETA band]. Our terms are no refunds once production's committed, which I know isn't what you want to hear. What I can do is [real alternative, e.g. priority dispatch / a goodwill add-on / hold it for a friend]. If the wait's the problem, tell me and I'll do what I actually can."]
>
> Either way, you'll get a real answer from me, not a runaround.

**Why.** A refund request is a test of whether you're a person or a wall. If you refund, do it fast and clean, that's the cheapest goodwill you'll ever buy. If you can't, never hide behind the policy: state it plainly, then immediately offer the real thing you *can* do. The backer who gets a straight no plus a genuine alternative disputes far less than the one who gets a warm non-answer.

### 10. Chargeback threat (the 11pm email)

> [firstName] — I get it, and before you go to your card issuer, give me one shot to sort this directly, because I can actually move faster than a dispute can.
>
> Where your order really is: [stage]. Straightest timing I have: [ETA band]. If that's not good enough, tell me what would make this right and I'll tell you honestly whether I can do it.
>
> Reply straight to me. I'm a person, not a ticket queue, and I'd rather fix this than fight it.

**Why.** A chargeback threat is usually a plea for a human, not a decision. Beat the bank to it by being faster and more personal than a dispute process. Never argue the wait. Ask what would make it right, because half the time the answer is smaller than you feared.

### 11. Angry public comment or DM

> [firstName] — you're right to be frustrated, and I'm not going to get defensive about it in the comments.
>
> I just sent you a DM with exactly where your order stands and what I can do about it. Let's sort it there. Whatever's fair, I want to get you to it.

**Why.** In public, you acknowledge and move it private in two sentences, no explaining, no excuses where other backers are reading. The goal isn't to win the thread. It's to be visibly reasonable to the fifty people watching, then do the real work in the DM.

### 12. "Is this a scam?" (trust has collapsed)

> [firstName] — fair question, and I'd rather answer it than be offended by it.
>
> I'm [name], I ran [real credibility, e.g. "fulfillment for a physical-goods brand for years before this"], and [product] is real and [stage]. Here's a photo of where it actually is: [attach]. Window from here is [ETA band].
>
> I know words are cheap this far in. Reply and I'll get you whatever proof you need.

**Why.** You cannot argue someone out of "this is a scam." You can only show them a real photo of a real thing and a real person's name. Offense reads as guilt. Proof and a name read as a founder who's still here.

## Special situations

### 13. Address or detail change (a calm, logistical ask)

> Hi [firstName] — got it, I've updated your shipping address to [new address]. It'll go out to the new one.
>
> No impact on your timing. Current window is still [ETA band]. Anything else, just say.

**Why.** This is the one most people get wrong: an address change is a calm, happy ticket, and answering it with wait-anxiety language ("I know you've waited so long, I'd rather sort this than have it go to your bank") is bizarre and alarming. Match the energy of the actual request. A logistical question gets a logistical answer.

### 14. Partial or split shipment

> Hi [firstName] — heads up, your order's shipping in two parts. [Item A] goes out now, [item B] follows once [reason, e.g. "the second colorway clears production"] in roughly [ETA band].
>
> You'll get tracking for the first part [when]. I'll flag the second the moment it moves.

**Why.** Splits confuse people and confusion breeds tickets. Name both parts, both reasons, both windows, in one message, so they're not left wondering why half their order arrived.

### 15. Backer wants to cancel (changed their mind, not angry)

> Hi [firstName] — no problem at all, and no hard feelings. [If you can cancel: "I've cancelled it and [refund detail]." If it's too far in: "It's already [stage], so I can't pull it back, but here's what I can do: [real option]."]
>
> Thanks for giving it a shot. If your timing changes down the line, you know where to find me.

**Why.** A calm cancel is not a dispute, so don't treat it like one. Make it easy, thank them, leave the door open. Fighting a friendly cancel is how you turn it into an angry one.

### 16. "Where's my tracking?" (and there isn't one yet)

> Hi [firstName] — no tracking number yet, and I won't send you a fake one to buy time.
>
> The reason there's no tracking is that it hasn't shipped, and it hasn't shipped because [stage]. The moment a real number exists, it lands in your inbox automatically. Current window: [ETA band].

**Why.** The honest version of "no tracking" beats every workaround. People ask for tracking because they want proof of motion. Give them the real proof, the current stage, instead of a number that leads to a "label created" page that never updates and makes it worse.

### 17. Post-delivery, close the loop

> [firstName] — it's there. Thank you for the patience that got it to you, genuinely. That wait was longer than I wanted for you.
>
> If anything's not right with it, reply and I'll make it right. And if it is right, I'd love to know what you think of it.
>
> [Optional goodwill: "There's a [small perk, e.g. discount code / digital extra] on your account as a thank-you for waiting."]

**Why.** The wait ends but the relationship doesn't have to. The backer who waited 100 days and got a human thank-you at the end is your next campaign's first backer. Closing the loop is where a delayed order turns into a repeat customer.
