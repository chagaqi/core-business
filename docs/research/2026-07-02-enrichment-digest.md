## RISKS
- Benchmark-as-promise trap (proof-only violation): displaying 'industry CSAT is 85%' next to a merchant's tiles can read as a promised outcome. Keep third-party numbers in clearly-cited info popovers, never in the same visual register as merchant data, and add benchmark-claim patterns to proof-lint's review checklist.
- Cross-vertical benchmark trap: Gorgias's own data shows FRT varies 5.5x and tickets/100 orders 2.4x across verticals — quoting one universal number in the UI or outreach will look naive to a real support manager. Always anchor to 'your captured baseline' first and cite vertical-level ranges second.
- Do NOT build a chatbot/AI deflection agent this sprint: 60–90% AI deflection claims require knowledge-base investment and deep integration (enterprise median for tier-1 is ~41%), and an auto-answering bot undercuts Tideover's human-approved trust story. Deflection here comes from the proactive status page; measure that instead.
- Do NOT build real WFM: forecasting + scheduling + occupancy software is enterprise scope. For a 1–2 person PH tier, the cohort forecast panel plus the existing twice-daily SLA windows is the whole requirement; anything more burns the 3 days.
- QA theater: a 15–20-question scorecard will crush VA throughput and stall grading entirely — MaestroQA's own guidance is few criteria organized in sections with auto-fail reserved for the deadly stuff. Ship 4 dimensions, 2 of them auto-scored, or the QA module becomes shelfware.
- 'Self-improving' scope creep into ML: at presale-merchant ticket volumes, per-template outcome stats are small-sample; without an explicit sample-size floor the champion/challenger will thrash on noise and the 'algorithm' becomes a random-number generator. Keep it deterministic, thresholded, and logged.
- Lingo misuse in outreach: a Head of CX will smell a fake in one sentence if terms are wrong (deflection ≠ automation rate; FCR ≠ one-touch; IQS ≠ CSAT; SLA attainment ≠ FRT). The training doc must carry exact definitions and Dylan should drill the 10 diagnostic openers before any call goes out.
- NPS in the product is a wrong turn: it is a brand-loyalty metric that moves quarterly and is meaningless mid-wait; putting it in the dashboard invites a metric Tideover cannot move in a pilot. Use CSAT/CES per interaction and leave NPS out entirely.
## GLOSSARY
- WISMO — 'Where Is My Order': the ticket type asking for order status; typically 25–40% of all ecommerce support volume and 50%+ in peak periods. The exact volume Tideover exists to prevent.
- FRT (First Response Time) — time from ticket creation to the first reply; ecommerce email median is ~5 hours, top-quartile merchants under 1 hour; Zendesk's ladder: ≤12h decent, ≤4h better, ≤1h best.
- TTR (Time To Resolution) — total time from ticket open to fully closed; teams report the median to dampen outliers, and it captures the whole interaction rather than just the first reply.
- FCR (First Contact Resolution) — % of issues fully resolved in one interaction; ecommerce average ~70–75%, top performers 80–85%.
- One-touch ticket — a ticket closed with a single agent reply; the helpdesk-report sibling of FCR and a favorite efficiency stat of team leads.
- Reopen rate — % of 'solved' tickets the customer replies to again; the honesty check on whether replies actually resolved anything (Tideover's playbook outcome signal).
- CSAT (Customer Satisfaction Score) — post-interaction 1–5 survey reported as % positive; 75–85% is considered good, top ecommerce teams target 90%+; remarkably stable across verticals.
- CES (Customer Effort Score) — 'how easy was it to get your issue resolved?' on a 1–7 scale; underused in ecommerce and arguably more predictive of repeat purchase than CSAT.
- NPS (Net Promoter Score) — 0–10 'would you recommend us' minus detractors; brand-level and slow-moving (retail/ecommerce averages roughly 45–59). Not a per-ticket metric.
- IQS (Internal Quality Score) — the internal grade of support replies against a QA rubric, expressed as a %; the industry benchmark is ~88% (Klaus survey of 4,000+ support pros). Says 'quality' without waiting for customer surveys.
- QA scorecard / rubric — the questionnaire + grading scheme a reviewer applies to an agent's interaction; typical dimensions: tone/empathy, product knowledge/accuracy, resolution, process adherence.
- 4C framework — MaestroQA's rubric template: Communication, Connection (customer experience), Correct & Complete content, Compliance & security.
- Auto-fail — a scorecard section where a single violation zeroes the entire score regardless of other answers (e.g., promising a hard delivery date, leaking PII).
- Calibration (session) — reviewers independently grade the same tickets then reconcile scores so grading means the same thing across people; the standard weekly ritual for QA programs and BPO oversight.
- Macro / saved reply / canned response — a pre-written reply template with variables ({first_name}, order status) auto-filled; the atomic unit of professional support work.
- Macro naming convention — tag-prefixed, searchable names (e.g. 'WISMO/day-60/anxious' or Gorgias's 'PS - offer type') so agents pull the right macro before finishing reading the ticket; one macro per scenario, not multi-option monsters.
- Contact driver / reason code — the taxonomy tag recording WHY a customer wrote in (wismo, refund, address change…); drives the Pareto report that decides where a CX leader invests.
- Tickets per 100 orders (contact rate) — support volume normalized to order volume; varies ~2.4x across verticals, so pros compare against vertical peers, never a universal number.
- Deflection rate — % of contacts resolved without a human (help center, status page, bot); 15–30% is a typical ecommerce baseline without AI, 40–65% with well-configured AI.
- SLA (Service Level Agreement) — committed response/resolution targets per channel and priority tier; 'SLA attainment' = % of tickets meeting target, 'breach' = missing one.
- Escalation matrix — the documented map of what escalates to whom and when; 'functional' escalation goes to a specialist, 'hierarchical' escalation goes up the management chain.
- Support tiers (0/1/2/3) — tier 0: self-serve (FAQ, order tracking); tier 1: standard queries (status, policy); tier 2: complex cases (carrier claims, exceptions, partial refunds); tier 3: high-risk (fraud, legal, chargeback threats, VIPs).
- Triage — first-pass sorting of the incoming queue by type, priority, and routing before anyone answers anything.
- Backlog — open, unanswered tickets; the first number a team lead checks every morning.
- AHT (Average Handle Time) — average working minutes an agent spends per ticket; the input to staffing and cost-per-ticket math (Tideover's PH-tier plan assumes ~6 min/presale ticket).
- Occupancy / utilization — % of paid agent time spent actively handling tickets; WFM keeps it below roughly 85–90% to prevent burnout and SLA collapse.
- WFM (Workforce Management) — the function that forecasts ticket volume and schedules agents so SLAs are met; at scale it is a dedicated role.
- Head of CX / Director of Support — owns support strategy, metrics, and budget; the buyer persona at a 50-person brand and the person Dylan's cold outreach will reach.
- Team Lead / Supervisor — runs the day-to-day: queue coverage, coaching, quality reviews, escalations, performance feedback.
- QA Analyst — grades sampled tickets against the scorecard, runs calibration sessions, reports IQS and coaching themes.
- BPO (Business Process Outsourcing) — an outsourced support provider; evaluated on SLA attainment, IQS, and AHT — exactly the controls Tideover's managed PH tier should publish.
- Knowledge base / Help center — public self-service documentation; the classic tier-0 deflection layer (Tideover's status page plays this role for presale orders).
- Proactive support — contacting the customer before they write in (delay notices, status updates); the industry's name for what Tideover's day-stage cadence does, and the lever behind best-in-class WISMO rates below ~4%.

## GLOSSARY
- Backer — a person who pledges money to a crowdfunding campaign in exchange for a promised reward; treated as a project supporter, not a retail buyer, which is why platforms don't guarantee delivery or refunds.
- Pledge — the money a backer commits; on Kickstarter cards are charged only if the campaign hits its goal by the deadline, then funds clear over ~14 days while failed cards are retried.
- All-or-nothing — Kickstarter's model: reach the funding goal or nobody is charged and the creator gets nothing. Kickstarter takes 5% plus Stripe processing (~3% + $0.20 per pledge over $10).
- Fixed vs flexible funding — Indiegogo's two modes: fixed refunds everyone if the goal is missed; flexible lets the creator keep whatever was raised (and still owe the perks).
- Reward tier — the package a backer selects at a pledge level (e.g. '1x device, early-bird price'); it becomes the SKU at fulfillment time.
- Stretch goal — extra items/features unlocked as funding passes milestones; a classic source of scope creep and delivery delays.
- Add-on — an extra item bought on top of a pledge, usually selected later in the pledge-manager survey.
- Early bird — a discounted, limited tier for the first backers; conventionally ships in the first wave.
- Pledge manager — the post-campaign tool (BackerKit, PledgeBox, PledgeManager.com, Gamefound; Kickstarter now has a native one) that surveys backers for addresses and reward/add-on choices, charges for shipping/tax, and produces the fulfillment export. Note: Kickbooster is a referral/affiliate tool, NOT a pledge manager.
- Backer survey — the pledge manager's questionnaire; shipping addresses are collected (or re-confirmed) here rather than at pledge time because addresses go stale over a 60-120 day wait.
- Smoke test — BackerKit practice of sending the survey to a small slice (~5%) of backers first to catch pricing/SKU errors before the full send.
- Backer report — Kickstarter's CSV export of backer name, email, tier, and amount that creators feed into the pledge manager; KS limits how this data may be used (no unsolicited marketing).
- Late pledge / InDemand — continuing to take orders after the campaign ends: Kickstarter Late Pledges, Indiegogo InDemand (5% fee native, 8%+ for external campaigns), or the merchant's own Shopify preorder store — the moment the audience becomes Shopify customers, and Tideover's home turf.
- Pledge drop / failed payment — pledges lost when a backer's card fails during the post-campaign collection window; platforms retry for about a week, then creators chase or lose the pledge.
- WISMO — 'Where Is My Order?': the inquiry type that dominates presale support; the industry shorthand every support manager uses.
- Fulfillment wave / shipping wave — shipping in batches (early birds → main wave → late pledges, often split by region) instead of all at once; the standard operating pattern of crowdfunding 3PLs.
- 3PL — third-party logistics provider that warehouses and ships rewards; crowdfunding-savvy 3PLs (Fulfillrite, Spiral Galaxy, etc.) run regional hubs.
- EU-friendly shipping / regional hub — bulk-freighting inventory into an EU/UK warehouse so backers don't get surprise customs bills; 'EU-friendly' is a literal badge backers look for on campaign pages.
- IOSS — EU Import One-Stop Shop: collecting EU VAT upfront on parcels under €150 so packages clear customs without the backer being dunned; a top inquiry topic for EU backers.
- Chargeback / dispute — the buyer asks their card issuer to reverse the charge. On Shopify Payments the merchant pays a $15 fee (US; returned if won), gets roughly 7-21 days to submit evidence, and resolution can take up to ~120 days.
- Reason code 13.1 — Visa's 'Merchandise/Services Not Received' code (Mastercard 4855, Amex 155, Discover 4755): filable up to 120 days after the EXPECTED delivery date, max 540 days after the transaction, after a mandatory 15-day issuer wait — the rule that keeps a presale cohort's GMV exposed for months.
- Representment — the merchant's evidence-backed rebuttal to a chargeback; on Kickstarter, KS handles representment and bills the creator if the dispute is lost.
- Reserve — funds held back against future refunds/chargebacks; Indiegogo automatically holds 5% of funds for 6+ months rolling.
- Update (project update) — the public broadcast post on the campaign page; the norm is monthly minimum, ~3-weekly when stable, weekly during problems. Backers can formally 'request an update' after 4 weeks of silence and KS can restrict chronically silent creators.
- FRT / SLA / CSAT / deflection / macro / triage / escalation / FCR / backlog — helpdesk-manager vocabulary: first response time; the promised response window; customer satisfaction score; tickets prevented by self-service (the status page's job); a canned reply template; sorting the queue by urgency; routing to a senior human; first-contact resolution; the unresolved-ticket pile.

## GLOSSARY
- WISMO — 'Where Is My Order?': the ticket category (order-status inquiries) that dominates presale support volume and precedes refunds/chargebacks when handled badly.
- Webhook — an automated HTTP request one system fires at another's URL when an event happens (e.g. 'ticket created'); push-based, no polling, no login shared.
- Gorgias HTTP integration — Gorgias's name for outbound webhooks: configured under Settings → Account → HTTP integration, fires on ticket events, request body is templated with {{ticket.*}} variables, 5-second timeout with 3 retries.
- Zendesk trigger — an if-this-then-that rule inside Zendesk; conditions (like 'Tags contains presale') gate actions, including 'Notify active webhook' which sends a custom JSON body built from placeholders.
- HMAC signature — a cryptographic stamp (e.g. X-Gorgias-Signature, X-Zendesk-Webhook-Signature) computed from the payload plus a shared secret, proving a webhook really came from the vendor and wasn't tampered with.
- OAuth scope — the specific permission an app requests when a merchant clicks 'install' (e.g. tickets:read); broader scopes = more data exposed = scarier consent screen.
- Forwarding alias / auto-BCC — a mail-filter rule in Gmail/Outlook/helpdesk that sends a copy of matching messages to another address; the zero-trust integration since no credentials change hands and deleting the filter revokes everything.
- SPF/DKIM — DNS records a domain owner publishes to authorize a third party (Klaviyo, Postmark, Tideover) to send email as their domain; delegation via DNS record, never via password.
- Inbound parse — a service (Postmark inbound, Mailgun Routes, Cloudflare Email Workers) that receives an email and converts it into a structured JSON webhook your app can process.
- Protected customer data (Shopify) — Shopify's framework where customer PII in API responses is redacted by default; public apps must apply for access in the Partner Dashboard and accept data-protection obligations.
- Custom app vs public app (Shopify) — a custom app is created by the merchant inside their own admin with self-chosen scopes and no Shopify review; a public app is distributed via the App Store and must pass review including protected-data approval.
- API token / key — a long-lived credential for server-to-server calls; in Gorgias it's per-user via Basic auth and inherits that user's permissions, so a least-permission bot user scopes the blast radius.
- Idempotency — designing an endpoint so the same event delivered twice (vendor retries) produces one ticket, typically by deduping on the external event/ticket ID.
- Fast-ACK — acknowledging a webhook with HTTP 200 immediately and doing the heavy processing asynchronously, so vendor timeouts (Gorgias: 5s) never mark deliveries failed.
- Data minimization — sending/storing only the fields needed for the job; at Rung 2 the merchant enforces it themselves via the webhook body template.
- Revocability — how fast and unilaterally a merchant can cut an integration off; the ladder's currency of trust (delete filter → deactivate webhook → reset API key → uninstall app).

## GLOSSARY
- WISMO — 'Where Is My Order?': the ticket type that dominates presale support queues and the volume Tideover exists to deflect.
- CSAT (one-tap) — customer satisfaction captured as a single thumbs up/down or 1-5 tap right after an interaction; expressed as % positive. Zendesk's solved-ticket thumbs survey is the canonical binary version.
- Reopen rate — % of solved tickets a customer's reply reopens; under 10% is the commonly accepted target, under 5% is excellent. A reopened ticket means the reply didn't actually resolve the worry.
- First response time (FRT) — elapsed time from ticket creation to first human-approved reply; Tideover already records firstResponseSec and baselines median FRT per merchant.
- Append-only event ledger — a table you only ever insert into, never edit; all displayed stats are recomputed from it, making every number auditable back to raw events (the proof-only mechanism).
- Attribution window — the fixed period after a sent reply (or order) during which an outcome (reply, reopen, refund, chargeback) is credited to it; e.g. refund-within-14-days, chargeback-within-60-days.
- Reason code 13.1 / 120-day rule — Visa's 'Merchandise/Services Not Received' dispute; the customer has 120 days from the date they last EXPECTED delivery (max 540 from transaction) to file, which is why presale chargeback stats stay open for months.
- Multi-armed bandit — an algorithm that splits traffic among variants while learning, shifting more traffic to better performers instead of waiting for a fixed A/B test to end.
- Epsilon-greedy — the simplest bandit: with probability epsilon (e.g. 0.2) pick a random variant (explore), otherwise pick the current best (exploit).
- Thompson sampling — a Bayesian bandit: each variant keeps a Beta(1+successes, 1+failures) distribution; you draw one random sample per variant and send the highest draw. It self-balances exploration and exploitation as evidence accumulates.
- Beta distribution / conjugate prior — the probability curve over 'true success rate' used by Thompson sampling; 'conjugate' means an observed success or failure updates it by simply incrementing one of two counters.
- Cold start / pooling — the no-data-yet problem for a new merchant or variant; solved by pooling stats across merchants for shared library scripts until local volume (n>=30) takes over.
- Golden test / regression suite — a frozen set of input scenarios with human-approved expected outputs; every code change re-runs them, so a behavior change that violates the spec fails the build before ship.
- Invariant (property) test — an assertion that must hold for ALL inputs (e.g. 'no draft ever contains a hard date'), checked by sweeping the whole input space rather than sampled examples.
- Edit rate — % of a draft's characters operators change before sending; a leading indicator of script quality that accrues immediately, long before refund outcomes mature.
- Counterfactual replay — re-running a candidate selection policy over historical logged events to see what it WOULD have chosen; a cheap pre-launch sanity check on the policy.
- Kill switch / feature flag — a per-merchant setting (selectionPolicy) that instantly reverts the system to deterministic defaults with no deploy.
- Small-N humility — refusing to display or act on a rate until sample size is sufficient (show 'collecting data, n=X' below n=20; no selection weighting below n=30).

- BPO (Business Process Outsourcing) — the industry term for firms like TalentPop/PartnerHero that rent you trained support staff; 'we use a BPO' is how larger brands describe outsourced support.
- SLA (Service Level Agreement) — the contractual response-time promise (e.g., 'presale tickets answered within two daily windows'); the first thing a CS manager at a larger company asks about.
- QA scorecard — the rubric a support team lead uses to grade a sample of agent replies (accuracy, tone, policy compliance); sampling 5–10% of tickets weekly is standard practice.
- Escalation matrix — the written rules for when a frontline agent must hand a ticket up (e.g., legal threat, chargeback threat, refund above $X) and to whom, within what time.

- Procurement threshold — the spend level (often ~$500–$1,000/mo at SMBs) below which a manager can buy on a card without triggering formal vendor review; pricing the pilot under it is a legitimate speed tactic.
- Deflection — tickets prevented because the customer self-served (e.g., via the /status page); the metric that justifies the status page + widget to a CS manager.

## GLOSSARY
- WISMO — 'Where is my order?': the ticket category covering order-status inquiries; benchmarks put it at 20–40% of all ecommerce support tickets, past 50% in peak periods, and far higher for presale brands mid-wait.
- FRT (first response time) — minutes/hours from a customer's message to the first human or approved reply; the leading indicator Tideover baselines and measures.
- AHT (average handle time) — minutes an agent spends resolving one ticket; drives support-cost math (~6 min/presale ticket with engine drafts in the Tideover plan).
- Deflection — a customer self-serving (e.g., checking the status page) instead of creating a ticket; measured as tickets avoided per 100 orders.
- Macro / canned response — a saved reply template in a helpdesk; the thing merchants think solves WISMO until day 60, when canned reads as 'we've checked out.'
- CSAT — post-ticket satisfaction rating (usually 1–5); the score support managers at larger companies are comped on, alongside FRT and SLA attainment.
- SLA (service-level agreement) — the promised response window (e.g., 'first reply within 4 business hours'); larger CS teams speak in SLA attainment percentages.
- Chargeback / dispute — a bank-forced payment reversal; costs the merchant a $15–30 processor fee but ~$128 all-in (Mastercard 2025), plus the product and the customer.
- INR ('item not received') — the card-network dispute reason code (Visa 13.1) that long fulfillment waits directly trigger; a top ecommerce dispute reason.
- Friendly fraud — a customer disputing a legitimate charge (e.g., impatience or buyer's remorse dressed as non-delivery); INR is the easiest code to abuse.
- VAMP (Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program) — Visa's combined fraud+dispute ratio monitoring; enforcement began Oct 2025 and the threshold drops to 1.5% for US/CA/EU/APAC in April 2026, making acquirers stricter with long-delivery merchants.
- Pledge manager — post-campaign software (BackerKit is the leader) that surveys backers for addresses/options and sells add-ons; BackerKit charges a sliding ~2% campaign fee plus 3.5% on pledge-manager transactions.
- Late pledge — a preorder placed after the campaign ends, through the pledge manager; ships on a different clock than original backer orders.
- Backer survey — the pledge-manager questionnaire collecting shipping address and reward choices; unanswered surveys are a classic fulfillment blocker.
- FTC Mail/Internet Order Rule ('30-day rule', 16 CFR 435) — US merchants must ship within the stated window (30 days if none stated) or send a delay notice offering a revised date and a full-refund cancel option; silence only counts as consent for delays ≤30 days.
- Per-resolution pricing — helpdesk AI billing per conversation the bot fully resolves; Gorgias charges $0.90–1.00/resolution on top of ticket fees, with $1.50 overages.
- Confidence band — Tideover's delivery language ('currently tracking for weeks 9–11') — a windowed estimate that is never a hard date, enforced in code by assertNoHardDate.
- Baseline — the merchant's pre-Tideover metrics (median FRT, WISMO/100 orders, tickets/week, repeat-WISMO %) captured at day 0; every result is reported as a delta against it.
- Founding/design partner — an early customer who gets the product free or discounted in exchange for real-inbox access, feedback, and approval-gated case-study rights.
- Escalation — routing a ticket past the standard queue to a human or senior operator; Tideover fast-lanes hostile and chargeback-threat sentiment automatically.

## GLOSSARY

- Smoke test — a fast 'is it alive?' check run against the live site right after deploying: key pages load, key content (like the confidence band) renders, nothing forbidden (like a hard date) appears.
- Triage — the ritual of processing new issues: remove duplicates, apply labels, rank by priority, flag anything waiting on the founder; keeps the backlog honest and small.
- Label taxonomy — the small fixed set of tags on issues (bug / feature / research / decision / blocked-on-dylan / released); small on purpose so filtering and rituals stay simple.
- Conventional commits — commit messages prefixed with a type (feat:, fix:, engine:, copy:) so history is scannable and the changelog can be generated from it automatically.
- Changelog — a human-readable, dated list of what shipped in each release, newest first; the product's public memory (Keep a Changelog is the standard format).
- gh CLI — GitHub's command-line tool; how AI agents file issues, open PRs, apply labels, and read CI results without ever opening a browser.

## GLOSSARY
- WISMO — 'Where Is My Order': the ticket category (status-inquiry emails/chats) that Tideover exists to prevent; support teams track it per 100 orders.
- FRT (First Response Time) — minutes/hours from a ticket arriving to the first human reply; median FRT is the headline speed KPI in every helpdesk and already on Tideover's dashboard.
- Macro — a saved reply template in helpdesks like Gorgias, fired by a button or Cmd+1/2/3; the industry word for what Tideover's draft variants are.
- Triage — the fast sorting pass over an inbox (by urgency, sentiment, owner) before anyone writes replies; 'keyboard-first triage' means doing it without touching the mouse.
- Deflection — the share of would-be tickets answered by self-serve (status page, widget, FAQ) so no agent ever touches them; Tideover's status page is a deflection surface.
- SLA (Service Level Agreement) — an internal or contractual response-time target, e.g. 'first reply within 4 business hours'; larger support teams manage queues by SLA breach risk.
- CSAT — the post-resolution satisfaction rating ('how did we do?'); the standard quality KPI alongside FRT at teams with dedicated managers.
- Views / split inbox — pre-filtered queues (by tag, channel, urgency) that helpdesks use to route work to specific agents; Tideover's priority queue is a single opinionated view.
- Command palette — the Cmd+K overlay (Superhuman, Linear, Slack) listing every action with its shortcut shown inline, which teaches shortcuts passively.
- Labor illusion / operational transparency — Buell & Norton (Management Science, 2011): when customers can see the effort being exerted for them, they value the service more and tolerate waits better — the research basis for the workshop-update feed.
- Goal-gradient effect — people feel better and push harder as they perceive themselves nearing a goal; why an elapsed-days progress bar makes a 90-day wait feel shorter.
- Idleness aversion — people tolerate waits far better when given something to engage with than when left idle (one of Uber's three wait-psychology levers).
- Time-to-value / aha moment — minutes from signup to first 'it works'; best-in-class self-serve SaaS lands it under 5 minutes, which is what demo-tour-first onboarding buys.
- Backer update — Kickstarter's creator-to-backer post; platform guidance: steady cadence (roughly every 2-3 weeks when stable), MORE frequent during delays, photos as proof of work — silence, not delay, is what breaks trust.
- North-star / hero metric — the single dominant number in a dashboard's top-left position that non-analyst users read first; everything else supports or drills down from it.
