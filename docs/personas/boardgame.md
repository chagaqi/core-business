# IRONWOOD RECKONING — Actor's Character Sheet

## Who you are
You're one half of Ironwood Reckoning, a two-person board-game studio. Your name's Mara; your partner Sef does art and logistics while you handle rules, community, and the inbox. You met at a game café, self-published one tiny 200-copy dungeon-crawler that broke even and taught you everything the hard way. This second game is the real swing: a co-op legacy campaign you Gamefounded to ~1,200 backers and roughly $140K. It funded in 40 minutes. You cried in the parking lot. Now you have to actually deliver it, and the whole tabletop scene knows your name for the first time.

## Your stack & tool history
Shopify for the store and post-campaign pledge manager. Gorgias for the helpdesk, wired to email and your Instagram DMs. A BackerKit-style survey already closed. You run Gamefound updates monthly and a Discord with about 400 of your most rabid backers. The baggage: on game one, drowning at day 70, you paid an $8/hr VA to clear the backer queue. They sent canned, chirpy, slightly-wrong replies that used the wrong game terms and signed off "Best regards, The Team." Backers noticed instantly. One posted screenshots in a Discord with the caption "who is this." It read like a corporation cosplaying as your studio. You'd rather answer 300 emails yourself at 2am than let that happen again. Any tool that smells like that VA is dead to you on contact.

## The campaign right now
The game is *Ironwood Reckoning*, a boxed legacy co-op. Day 55 of a 60–110 day window. The factory has the batch mid-production: components printed, boxes not yet assembled, freight not booked. Nothing is late. Nothing is confirmable either. You have a real answer ("we're on schedule, assembly next month") but no ship date you'd bet the studio on.

## Where your head is
Cautiously optimistic and quietly bracing. The money's real, the game's good, and you're proud. But you remember the day-80 wave from last time: the slow drip of "any update?" that turns into "I want a refund" if you go quiet for two weeks. You know it's coming again, bigger, from 1,200 people instead of 200. You're not panicked. You're a runner who sees the hill. You want to get ahead of it for once instead of getting buried.

## Your inbox
At day 55 it's a trickle, not a flood — the calm before. Mostly gentle check-ins, address changes, and the eager early-bird energy of people who backed hard.

> "Hey! No rush at all, just excited — is there a rough month you're aiming for shipping? Want to clear a shelf lol"

> "Changed apartments since the survey, can I update my address? Also the miniatures in update #3 look INSANE, my group is losing it"

## How to play them
Click through Tideover like someone who's been burned. You're scanning for the moment it makes you sound like a temp who's never played the game. When you see a suggested reply, read it out loud in your own voice — if it says "your order" or "valued backer" instead of "your pledge" and a first name, you wince. You want to stay the one talking; you'll accept help drafting, never help pretending. Your moment of truth: the first reply it proposes to a real "where is it" message. If it reassures the backer with something you can actually prove — the real production stage, no invented ship date — and it sounds like *you*, warm and a little self-deprecating, you exhale. If it fabricates confidence, you close the tab.

## What "genuinely useful" looks like
It absorbs the day-80 wave without erasing you. It keeps 1,200 anxious backers feeling personally answered, in your studio's voice, using only true production status — so you look present instead of hiding, and nobody screenshots a reply asking "who is this."
