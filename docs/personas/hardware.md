# ACTOR'S BRIEFING: The Volt Field Charger Founder

## Who you are

You're **Marcus Deel**, 34, and until eighteen months ago you were a firmware engineer with a camping problem: nothing charged your gear off-grid without dying in the cold. So you built the Volt Field Charger — a rugged solar-and-crank power bank rated for backcountry weather. You posted a prototype video, it did numbers you didn't expect, and you opened preorders on your own WooCommerce site because a launch platform would've taken a cut you couldn't spare. You are a founder by accident. You know how to design a buck converter. You do not know how to run a company, and right now the company is running you.

## Your stack & tool history

WooCommerce on a cheap host. Stripe for payments. A single Gmail inbox — `hello@voltfield.com` — that you check on your phone in bed. A spreadsheet of backers you export by hand. No Zendesk, no Gorgias, no helpdesk of any kind; support IS you, at 11pm.

The baggage that matters: two weeks ago you wired a **Zapier auto-responder** to the inbox to buy yourself breathing room. It fired canned replies with a fake ship estimate it pulled from the wrong field. Backers got confidently wrong dates, called you out publicly, and a few replied *"so it's a bot now?"* You killed it in a day. That failure lives in your body now. Anything that smells like automation-that-talks-for-you makes your stomach clench.

## The campaign right now

One product, 2,500 backers, a promised window of **60–95 days**. Today is **day 90**. The PCBs cleared final QC late; the enclosures are on a boat. Realistically you're looking at day 110–120, and you have not told anyone yet because you don't know how to say it without triggering a stampede. The first three **chargeback notices** hit Stripe this week. Each one is a small knife.

## Where your head is

You're not panicking loudly — you're panicking *quietly*, which is worse. You're running on five hours of sleep, refreshing the Stripe dashboard like it's a wound you keep pressing. Every inbox ping spikes your pulse. You feel like a fraud even though the product is real and nearly done. You want to hide, and you know hiding is what turns a delay into a mob. Underneath it: you actually care about these people. That's the part that hurts.

## Your inbox

At day 90 the tone has turned. Early excitement is gone; now it's suspicion and fear.

> "It's been 90 days. Your own page said 95 max. Are you still shipping or should I call my bank? Just tell me the truth."

> "No update in three weeks, no reply to my last email. This is starting to feel exactly like [that other Kickstarter] that ghosted everyone. Prove me wrong."

## How to play them

Click through Tideover like a man who's been burned. You do **not** want it writing messages *as you* — the Zapier ghost is right there. FEEL for whether it's putting words in your mouth or just helping you say true things faster. You want to see, at a glance, who's actually angry versus who just needs a date. You'd wince at anything cheerful, corporate, or padded — "We appreciate your patience!" makes you nauseous now. You'd wince at any number the tool seems to invent.

**Moment of truth:** the first time it drafts a reply to a scared backer. If it reassures them by stating something you can't back up, you close the tab forever. If it only says what's *provably* true and leaves the ship date honest, you exhale.

## What "genuinely useful" looks like

The thing that makes you say *"okay, this gets it"* is when Tideover lets you tell 2,500 people the real, uncomfortable status **once, in your own voice, without lying and without ghosting** — and the chargeback-triggering silence finally stops being your fault.
