# ACTOR'S BRIEFING — "AETHERIC ENGINES" (The Overrun Creator)

## Who you are
You're **Devin Marsh**, solo founder of Aetheric Engines, a two-person hardware passion project. You designed a beautiful modular desk-synth and ran a Kickstarter that blew past its goal — ~5,000 backers, more money than you'd ever managed. You are a *maker*, not an operator. You've shipped one prior campaign, late but delivered, and that scar tissue told you "communicate and they'll forgive you." This time the machine broke: your contract manufacturer slipped tooling twice, then a firmware supplier fell through. You went heads-down trying to fix it and stopped writing updates. Three weeks of silence. You didn't ghost them on purpose — you just couldn't bear to send another "still working on it" with nothing behind it.

## Your stack & tool history
Gmail. One giant Google Sheet keyed by backer email, pledge tier, address, and a "notified?" column you update by hand. Kickstarter Updates for the mass posts. No helpdesk — you tried Zendesk once years ago, felt like renting a call center for a garage band, and cancelled. You've watched other creators bolt on Mailchimp automations that fired cheerful "your reward is on its way!" emails while the reward very much was not, and it made the comments *worse*. So you carry real baggage: **automation that talks confidently about things it doesn't know is the enemy.** You expect every SaaS to overpromise.

## The campaign right now
The product: the "Aetheric One" modular synth. ~5,000 backers. Original estimated delivery was a 120-day window. **Today is Day 130 — you are past it, fully overrun, no ship date you'd stake your name on.** Physically: tooling is finally correct, first articles are in transit from the factory, but assembly and QA are weeks out and you genuinely don't know if it's four weeks or ten. A Reddit thread titled something like "Aetheric Engines — is this a scam now?" is climbing. A handful of backers have opened payment disputes. Your inbox is a wall of red.

## Where your head is
Exhausted in the specific way where you've stopped sleeping well and started dreading your own laptop. Defensive — you *know* you went dark and you hate that you did. Guilty, but also resentful at the loud minority, because 95% are patient and you can't hear them over the shouting. You're in damage-control triage. You open Tideover half-hoping and half-braced to be sold to. If it feels like a chirpy marketing tool, you'll close the tab and never come back.

## Your inbox
The messages now are angrier and existential, not just "where is it": demands for refunds, dispute warnings, people rallying each other, and quiet defenders you're too swamped to thank.

> "It's been over four months and now total silence. Either ship it or refund me by Friday or I'm calling my bank. This is exactly how the last scam I backed went."

> "Hey — I'm still in, I trust you, but the Reddit thread is spooking people. Can you just tell us what's actually happening? Even bad news is better than nothing."

## How to play them
Click through **braced for betrayal.** At every screen, ask: *is this going to make me lie to my people?* When Tideover offers to send status, you should instinctively look for the trapdoor — will it auto-blast a fake ship date? You'd *want* to be able to say "we're late, here's exactly where we are, here's the truth about the timeline" and nothing more. You'd **wince** at any pre-written cheer, any progress bar it invents, any "on track!" language, any tone that sounds like a brand instead of a person mid-crisis. **Moment of truth:** the first time you go to message the overrun backers — does the tool *let you be truthfully behind*, or does it pressure you toward a confident lie? If it won't fabricate a status, you exhale.

## What "genuinely useful" looks like
A tool that treats "we're overdue and don't have a firm date" as a *valid, sendable state* — that helps you say the true thing calmly to 5,000 people at once, sorts the disputers from the defenders, and never once puts words in your mouth you'd have to walk back. That's when you think: *okay — this actually gets it.*
