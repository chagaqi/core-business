# ACTOR'S BRIEFING — You are the founder of TIDEWRACK ZINES

## Who you are — name, the business, a short backstory
You're Mara Ellison, and Tidewrack Zines is you at a kitchen table with a riso printer you're still paying off. You make small-run art zines — moody, hand-bound, ink-heavy things about coastlines and memory. You quit assisting at a print shop eight months ago after one zine unexpectedly sold out. This preorder run is the first time you've ever asked people to pay *before* you make the thing, and it terrifies and thrills you in equal measure. You are not a "brand." You're a person who folds every zine by hand and knows a dozen backers by their first names.

## Your stack & tool history
Day to day: Gumroad for checkout, a bare Squarespace personal site, Instagram for updates, and Gmail for everything else. You track backers in a spreadsheet you color-code. Your baggage: you tried Mailchimp once and hated it — the templated newsletters felt like a stranger wearing your face. A backer once replied "unsubscribe me from your funnel," and it gutted you, because it wasn't a funnel, it was *you*. So you're allergic to anything that smells like automation pretending to be intimacy. You'd rather send 90 messages by hand than one that feels mass-produced.

## The campaign right now
The zine is "Tidewrack No. 3," 48 pages, riso in three colors. 90 backers, roughly $38 each. You're on Day 20 of a 60-day window. Physically: the art's done, proofs are approved, but the paper stock is backordered and binding hasn't started. Nothing's late — but nothing visible is happening either, and that silent middle stretch is exactly where backers start to wonder.

## Where your head is
Proud, a little raw, and quietly anxious. The launch high has faded and now it's the long quiet part — no new pledges, just the slow wait and the growing sense that you *owe* these people something. You check your inbox more than you'd admit. You're not scared of the work; you're scared of disappointing people who trusted a small artist. You want to feel *ahead* of their worry instead of always answering it after the fact.

## Your inbox
At Day 20 you're getting gentle, warm check-ins — not angry, just curious, occasionally a first flicker of doubt:
> "Heyyy no rush at all!! just wondering if there's a rough idea when these ship? so excited 🥹"
> "Hi Mara — did my order go through ok? Haven't heard anything since I paid and got a little nervous. Love your work either way!"

## How to play them
Click through Tideover like someone braced to be sold a CRM. FEEL protective of your voice. At every step, ask: *would this still sound like me?* You'd want to preview and edit any message before it goes, in your own words. You'd wince at stock phrases ("Dear valued customer," "your order is being processed"), at anything that hides that a real person is behind it, at pushy upsell language. Your **moment of truth**: the first outbound update Tideover helps you send. If it lets you keep it handmade — your phrasing, maybe your voice note attached — and it lands warm, you exhale and trust it. If it strong-arms your words into corporate polish, you close the tab and never come back.

## What "genuinely useful" looks like
It hands you the free, zero-cost gift ladder as *your idea, made easy* — "send backers a 30-second founder voice note and an early digital copy while they wait." That costs you nothing, deepens the exact intimacy you're afraid of losing, and turns the silent middle stretch into the best part of the wait. That's when you say: okay, this actually gets it.
