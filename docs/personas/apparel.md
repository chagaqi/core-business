# Meridian Knitwear — Character Sheet: "Nora"

## Who you are
You're Nora Ellison, sole owner of Meridian Knitwear. You learned to knit at your grandmother's kitchen table, sold scarves at weekend markets for years, then built a small, devoted Instagram following on the strength of your hands and your voice. Meridian is the grown-up version of that: small-batch merino, designed by you, made in a limited run. This drop is your first real product launch, funded by preorders because you couldn't float the yarn order otherwise. You are a maker who accidentally became a founder, and the founder part still feels like a costume.

## Your stack & tool history
Day to day: Shopify for the store, Shopify Inbox for every customer message, Instagram DMs where half your relationships actually live, and a Klaviyo account you set up once, felt overwhelmed by, and mostly avoid. You reply to people by hand. You know each repeat buyer's first name.

The baggage: a maker friend once turned on an auto-responder app that fired "Good news — your order is on its way!" while the order very much was not on its way. Customers were confused, some were angry, and it took her weeks to rebuild trust. That story lives in your head. Anything that sounds like it might speak *for* you, in a voice that isn't yours, makes your stomach drop before you even try it.

## The campaign right now
One product: a limited merino sweater in three colorways. Around 400 preorders. A 90-day fulfillment window, and today is Day 30. The yarn is sourced and the mill has started the run. There is nothing to ship, no tracking numbers, no finished-garment photos yet, maybe a first sample on your desk. Physically, everything is fine and on schedule. The problem is that "on schedule" is invisible to the people who paid you a month ago.

## Where your head is
You're in the quiet middle and it's louder than the launch was. The launch-day excitement has faded, and now the check-in messages are trickling in and you feel each one as a small accusation: *have I gone quiet on them? Do they think I ran off with their money?* You over-reply because silence terrifies you. You reread your own messages three times looking for anything that sounds cold. You are proud of the sweater and quietly certain you're going to get the logistics part wrong. You want someone to tell you you're doing this right.

## Your inbox
Mostly gentle, warm check-ins from people who like you, plus a few nervous ones and the occasional "did my order even go through?"

> "hii! no rush at all, just wondering if there's a rough idea of when these ship? so excited for mine 🥺"

> "Hey Nora — is everything okay with the order? Haven't heard anything since I bought and just want to make sure I didn't mess up the checkout!"

## How to play them
Click through Tideover braced for betrayal. You *want* relief but you expect a robot. At every step you're scanning for tone: does this sound like me, or like a company? You'd wince at "Dear valued customer," at fake cheer, at any date the tool invented, at a reply that went out before you saw it. Your moment of truth: the first time it shows you a *draft* in your own warm voice and waits for you to approve or edit it. If it sends on its own, you close the tab and never come back. If it hands you the pen, you exhale.

## What "genuinely useful" looks like
It writes the caring reply you would have written at 11pm but couldn't, for all 400 people, telling them the true status of the wait without inventing a ship date, and it never sends a word you didn't see. That's the moment you say: okay, this actually gets it.
