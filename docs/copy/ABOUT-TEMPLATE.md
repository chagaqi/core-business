> Purpose: lock the *skeleton* before writing a word. Every section below gives its job, its

> Fill it after the framework is approved.

## 1. The honest question: are big founder-story About pages actually a SaaS thing?

Dylan asked it straight, so here is the straight answer from the evidence. **A full
first-person founder *memoir* on a company's own About page is not a classic-SaaS convention.
It is a bootstrapper / indie move — and even the creator brands that have one usually keep it
*off* the company site.** Where a founder story shows up tracks the company's category and,
more importantly, whether it has *external proof* to stand on instead.

| Site | Category | Founder on the company About? | What carries trust instead | Src |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Basecamp / 37signals** | Bootstrapper SaaS | **YES — the About *is* a first-person letter**, ~80% founder, signed with a real personal email | Admission + structural facts (cited: "27 years profitable," "zero debt") | A1 |
| **Kit / Nathan Barry** | Creator SaaS | **SPLIT** — kit.com/about is founder-absent "we"; the full story is *quarantined* on nathanbarry.com (~3:1 founder:mission) | Dated specifics ($45M ARR) — but only on the *personal* page | A5 |
| **Gumroad / Sahil** | Indie / creator | **SPLIT / de-foundered** — product site is founder-invisible; ~7,400-word confession lives in personal essays | Confession-first + exact, un-rounded numbers | A8 |
| **Oatly** | DTC / CPG | **NO — mission-led, founder absent** (the Öste brothers appear only in press) | Mission + sourced data + a labeled "Big Fails!" block | A7 |
| **PostHog** | Dev-tool SaaS | **STAMP** — named + photo, but bio ~250 w vs ~1,200 w of philosophy (~1:5); the argument stays "we" | Transparency artifacts (public handbook/roadmap) + exact numbers | A6 |
| **Linear** | B2B SaaS | **NO** — founders are peers in a team grid; no bio, no "I", no story | Logos, "over 33,000 teams," investor names | A2 |
| **Stripe** | B2B fintech | **NO on marketing surfaces** — founders are silent headshots; founder voice is rationed to a yearly letter | Exact mega-numbers, logos, an honest-weaknesses careers section | A3 |
| **Mercury** | B2B fintech | **NO** — founder is deliberately kept off the About | Press wall, investor names, customer counts | A4 |

**What the table actually says:**

- **Only one company in the set — Basecamp — runs a big founder story on its own company
  About.** It is a bootstrapper, not a VC-scale SaaS.
- **The creator brands that *have* a big story (Kit, Gumroad) quarantine it** on a personal
  domain and keep the company About mission-led. The story does the work; it just doesn't do it
  on the money page.
- **Classic B2B SaaS and fintech (Linear, Stripe, Mercury) keep the founder minimal to absent**
  and lean on external proof: logos, big exact numbers, investor names, press.
- **PostHog is the middle path:** a named, *photographed* founder as a credibility *stamp*,
  while the argument itself stays institutional.
- **Honest correction to the premise:** Oatly, the DTC example, is *not* a founder-story brand
  at all — its About is mission-led and founder-absent (A7). So "big DTC founder story" is a
  weaker pattern than it feels; the real founder-story home is bootstrapper/indie, not DTC.

**The one variable that explains the whole table:** every founder-*minimal* company can afford
to hide its founder because it already has proof that speaks for it — 33,000 teams, $1.9tn,
300k customers, a press wall. **Take that external proof away and the founder is all that's
left to trust.** That is exactly Tideover's position.

### Verdict for Tideover specifically

Tideover is a **founder-led sale with no case studies**. That places it with *early*
Basecamp / early Kit — a company whose only credibility asset is the founder's lived
experience. So:

- **Yes, some founder presence is necessary here and earns trust.** With no logos, no numbers,
  no press, the honest first-person story is the substitute for the case studies Tideover
  cannot yet show. Hiding the founder (the Linear/Stripe/Mercury move) is a *luxury of the
  already-proven* and would leave the page with nothing to stand on.
- **But it need not be a memoir.** Gumroad's 7,400-word confessional and Basecamp's ~575-word
  letter are both "founder stories"; only the compact one fits a cold merchant on a 90-second
  read. The winning form for Tideover is a **compact first-person letter** — Basecamp's
  admission-based arc, delivered with PostHog's *stamp* restraint (named, pictured, credible,
  then hand off to the mechanism) — **~450-650 words, not 7,400.**

Net: put the named founder and the lived wait front and center, prove trust through admission +
structural fact + exact self-owned numbers, and stop there. The story earns the sale; it
doesn't have to *be* the whole site.

## 2. Recommended template site: **Basecamp / 37signals** ("Where we came from")

Template Tideover's About on the Basecamp founder letter.

- **It is the only model in the set that manufactures trust with *zero case studies* — Tideover's
  exact situation.** The whole About is a first-person admission ("clients noticed our failure
  first"), not a results wall. That is the precise problem Tideover has to solve, solved (A1).
- **Its three proof mechanisms are all things Tideover already owns and can use honestly:**
  narrative admission, *structural fact* (how you operate, not how big you are), and *opinion as
  the hype-substitute* — none of which require scale, logos, or numbers Tideover lacks (A1). The
  founder-minimal models (Linear/Stripe/Mercury) prove trust with exactly the assets Tideover
  doesn't have, so they are the wrong template even where the voice is admirable.
- **The register is the target voice:** plain-spoken, problem-first, low-hype, opinion-as-
  confidence — a bootstrapper talking, not a VC brand performing. That maps 1:1 onto "calm,
  trust-first."

**Runner-up: PostHog** (`/about`). Same instinct — a named, *photographed* founder as a trust
stamp plus a *confess-then-convert* structure and transparency-as-proof — but with the argument
kept institutional, which is the better model for the *home* and *how-it-works* pages where a
full letter is too much. Adopt its structure; strip its cocky, joke-heavy tone (wrong for an
anxious merchant).

**Two honest grafts onto the Basecamp spine** (because Tideover is proof-*less* where Basecamp
had a revenue fact to point at):
- Basecamp's section 6 proves fit with a hard fact ("revenue displaced the old business"). Tideover
  has no such fact yet → **graft Stripe's honest-limits / "here's what we can't prove yet" block**
  in its place (A3). Absence of proof, stated plainly, becomes the proof.
- **Open on a confession** the way Gumroad does (A8) — name the gap ("no case studies yet")
  before any claim, so everything after reads as pre-audited.

## 3. Voice rules (7) — distilled from Basecamp, adapted to proof-only

1. **Write like one email to one person.** First-person, contractions, direct "you," zero
   corporate mission-speak. If it sounds like a brand, cut it. (A1: the About *is* a founder
   talking.)
2. **Open every section on the problem or a plain fact — never on the product.** The reader
   should feel the wait before they hear the fix.
3. **Admission before claim.** Reject the myth, own the failure, name the gap *first*; honesty
   placed up front makes every later line read as already-audited. (A1 admission arc + A8
   confession-first.)
4. **Opinion is the hype-substitute.** Where a hype site would exaggerate, state a short belief,
   flat, no superlatives. Conviction reads as confidence, so you never have to shout. (A1
   "signals" move.)
5. **Proof = narrative + structural fact + exact numbers only.** How you operate (honest limits,
   no lock-in, the proof-only pledge) and exact self-owned figures — never scale flexes, invented
   benchmarks, borrowed logos, or testimonials you don't have. (A1 don't-transfer; A3/A5/A8.)
6. **Every number is exact and self-owned.** Un-rounded, in Dylan's own voice, flagged as his own
   account of his own business until real merchant results (shared with permission) replace it.
   Never round to look bigger. (A8 precision-as-sincerity; proof-only pledge.)
7. **Short declaratives carry the weight; sign with a real name and a real door.** Sentences run
   short-to-medium, hype stays low, and it closes with "— Dylan" and an actual way to reach him.
   (A1 personal-email signature.)

## 4. The ABOUT template — section by section (fill-in skeleton)

Reverse-engineered from Basecamp's 8-part About arc (A1), with the two proof-only grafts folded
in. Total target: **~450-650 words.** Leave all real copy bracketed until the framework is
approved.

Basecamp arc → Tideover mapping: thesis → founder intro → origin problem → turning point →
solution-from-pain → *[graft: honest-limits instead of revenue proof]* → responsibility/close.

---

### Section 1 — Thesis line
- **Words:** ~10-15
- **Function:** Frame the whole story as *necessity, not genius*, in one breath — so the reader
  knows this is a scar, not a pitch.
- **Says:** A single flat line, fragment-length, that names the wait as the reason the company
  exists. Calm, plain, zero adjectives. No product name yet, no benefit yet. Tone: matter-of-fact.
- **Structural move:** One-line thesis as a fragment (Basecamp's cited pattern: "Born out of
  desperate necessity"). Statement of origin, not a headline that sells.

### Section 2 — Founder intro
- **Words:** ~35-60
- **Function:** Put a named, real human on the page immediately, with the one credential that
  makes him the right person to have built this.
- **Says:** Direct address ("Hey there / I'm Dylan"), warm and un-grand. States the single
  load-bearing credential — *he lived the exact wait he's now selling relief from* — in plain
  terms. Photo sits here. Tone: intimate, unpolished, human. No résumé, no title-stacking.
- **Structural move:** Byline + one credibility anchor (Basecamp's cited "Hey there, I'm …" +
  tenure stat). Name → who I am → the one fact that earns the rest.
- **[COPY: _______ ]** · **[ASSET: founder headshot]**

### Section 3 — The lived problem (the wait)
- **Words:** ~140-200
- **Function:** Earn trust by admitting the pain he lived, in specifics — this is the section
  that replaces case studies.
- **Says:** The origin scene told as honest admission: the surge that looked like a win, then the
  60+ day wait he couldn't move, the WISMO inbox refilling every morning, the refund/chargeback
  pressure, the toll on sanity/family. Concrete and dated, not "it was hard." Exact self-owned
  numbers ($200K week 1, 60+ days, 2 years) in his voice. Tone: candid, humble, unflinching, low
  drama.
- **Structural move:** Reject the myth → own the mess → name the dropped balls (Basecamp's
  admission arc). Problem-first; the product does not appear yet.
- **[COPY: _______ ]**

### Section 4 — The bottom / the turning-point insight
- **Words:** ~70-110
- **Function:** Turn the pain into the *idea* — the moment the problem became a thing worth
  solving for others.
- **Says:** The "magic wand" moment in his own words — if the stress of the onslaught could just
  vanish, it would have saved his sanity, nervous system, family, and focus. That wish is where
  Tideover (the literal name — "tide the customers over") was born. Tone: reflective, quiet, the
  hinge of the letter.
- **Structural move:** Third-party-free turning point — an internal realization, not a testimonial
  (Tideover's honest substitute for Basecamp's "clients noticed before we did"). Wish → naming
  act → why the name is literal.
- **[COPY: _______ ]**

### Section 5 — What got built (the mechanism, as a byproduct of the scar)
- **Words:** ~110-170
- **Function:** Show the product emerging *organically from the pain*, and show that judgment was
  turned into software because instinct doesn't scale.
- **Says:** The honest admission that you shouldn't have to learn it the way he did — at 11pm on
  the 300th "where is my order," instinct fails. So the judgment lives in code: replies audited
  before they reach a customer, hard dates you can't control physically blocked, results measured
  only against your own store's baseline. Best-of-both: operator scar tissue + a measurement
  system. Tone: pragmatic, concrete, no hype. Short belief statements over adjectives.
- **Structural move:** List the real needs → the tool as byproduct (Basecamp's "product emerged,
  wasn't planned"). Mechanism shown as 2-3 flat capability lines, each a conviction.

  proof-via-product-surface]**

### Section 6 — The honest limits + proof-only pledge *(graft: Stripe honest-weaknesses)*
- **Words:** ~80-120
- **Function:** Convert the *absence* of proof into the reason to trust — the trust engine of a
  page with no case studies.
- **Says:** The one pledge that governs the whole site: proof only. No fabricated metrics, no fake
  logo wall, no "merchants love us" line he can't back. States plainly that the numbers above are
  his own account of his own business and stay the only numbers until real merchant results
  (shared with permission) replace them. Names what Tideover does *not* promise yet. Tone: direct,
  transparent, calm — honesty as a flex, never an apology.
- **Structural move:** Stripe's candid-weakness block (A3) in place of Basecamp's revenue proof:
  claim the limitation out loud → frame it as the reason to trust. Confess → convert (A6/A8).
- **[COPY: _______ ]**

### Section 7 — Who it's for + the invitation
- **Words:** ~70-110
- **Function:** Name the exact reader, then open a real door (founding-partner CTA).
- **Says:** Names the ICP precisely — Shopify merchants staring down a 60-120 day fulfillment
  wait, usually right after a crowdfunding campaign overperformed. Then the offer: a small group
  of founding partners, direct access to Dylan, a hand in shaping the product, and — only with
  their sign-off — their results becoming the proof this page runs on. Tone: warm, specific,
  low-pressure, generous.
- **Structural move:** Reframe success as duty + open a human door (Basecamp's responsibility
  close). "If that's you →" invitation, not a hard sell.

### Section 8 — Signature + human door
- **Words:** ~15-30
- **Function:** Close on a person, not a brand — a real way to reach him.
- **Says:** One line to the merchant mid-onslaught ("I know exactly where you're standing —
  write to me"), then the signature. Tone: quiet, direct, human.
- **Structural move:** Personal sign-off + real contact (Basecamp's signed-with-a-real-email
  close). "— Dylan" + a genuine reply-to.

## 5. Same method, whole site — how the template extends

The method is portable: **for each page, (a) reverse-engineer the reference surface into named
sections, (b) lock Section · Words · Function · Says · Structural move, (c) leave copy bracketed,
(d) apply the 7 voice rules and swap every scale/logo proof for structural-fact + honest-limits +
exact self-owned numbers.** Reference surface per page:

- **Home** → template on the **Basecamp homepage** skeleton (A1): felt category-promise hero →
  the wait named as shared pain → the mechanism → *structural-fact / stability block* → 3-5 belief
  statements → soft CTA. **Proof-only swap:** replace Basecamp's big-numbers scale flex with the
  proof-only structural-fact block + one exact self-owned number. Do **not** import the
  11-testimonial carousel or the "all Yes!" feature bravado (A1 don't-transfer).
- **How-it-works** → template on **Linear's** product-surface discipline (A2): one section per
  step of the mechanism, 4-8 word declarative headers, ~20-25 word blurbs, and proof-via-product-
  surface (show the real status/cockpit view; let the honest interface *be* the proof). **Swap:**
  no era-defining abstraction, no logo/investor credibility — open concrete on the merchant's wait.
- **Pricing** → template on **PostHog's pricing-philosophy** + **Stripe's FAQ objection-handling**
  (A6/A3): the D8 ladder (already decided in `mission-control/decisions/d8-pricing.html`) plus a
  "rules stated out loud" block where each rule is a wedge line, then a short honest-limits FAQ.
  **Swap:** flat/exact pricing with no per-ticket theatre; "no performance fees until real case
  studies exist" is the proof-only line. Same 7 voice rules carry over unchanged.

Once the About framework is approved, run the identical Section · Words · Function · Says ·
Structural-move pass on Home, How-it-works, and Pricing to template the whole site.
